Adding "Sports" expands the app from a pure exercise builder into a comprehensive physical activity tracker. Since your foundation already supports diverse disciplines like yoga, strength, and mobility, adding sports is a natural fit, provided the data model accommodates how sports are measured and displayed.

Here is how to seamlessly integrate sports into your current product architecture:

**1. Adapting the Data Taxonomy**
Your current data model uses filters like Discipline, Equipment, Target Muscle, and Joint. Sports operate differently because they are typically full-body, dynamic, and duration-based.

* **Discipline Addition:** Add "Sports" alongside your existing categories (Yoga, Strength, Mobility).


* **Measurement Override:** Unlike atomic exercises measured in sets, reps, or hold durations, sports must default to **Time/Duration**, Distance, or RPE (Rate of Perceived Exertion).


* **Hiding Anatomy Tags:** Use progressive disclosure to hide "Muscle" and "Joint" filters when the "Sports" discipline is selected. Playing basketball engages the whole body; forcing users to tag specific joints creates unnecessary friction and data clutter.



**2. Updating the Catalog Card UI**
Using the four-zone card architecture previously established for Svasthic, a sport card requires specific modifications to remain scannable:

* **Zone 2 (Header):** Clear titles like "Tennis (Singles)", "Bouldering", or "Swimming".
* **Zone 3 (Strategic Tagging):** Replace anatomical chips with format or intensity chips (e.g., ⏱️ Duration, 🫀 High Intensity, 🏃 Cardio) to prevent "chip soup".


* **Zone 4 (Action Bar):** The standard "+ Add" button remains, dropping the sport into the staging area or routine builder.



**3. Routine Assembly (The "Sandwich" Use Case)**
Treating a sport as an "Activity" unlocks a powerful routine-building feature. Users can build structured routines where the sport is the central event, bookended by your existing database of sequences.

* *Example Routine:* "Pre-Match Joint Mobility" (15 min sequence) + "Tennis" (90 min sport) + "Post-Match Static Stretch" (10 min sequence).



**4. Library Dashboard & Discovery**
In your newly redesigned Library dashboard:

* **Browse Grid:** Add a dedicated "Sports" or "Cardio" tile to your discipline browse grid, ensuring it sits clearly alongside Yoga and Strength.


* **Search Behavior:** Ensure the global search indexes sports accurately, allowing users to type "Running" alongside searches for "Kettlebell Goblet Squat".



**MVP Execution Strategy**
You do not need to build an exhaustive database of every sport globally for V1. Seed the shared catalog with 10-20 of the most common sports (Running, Cycling, Swimming, Football, Basketball, Tennis, Golf, etc.). Rely on the prominent **"+ Create Custom"** tile to empower users to add niche sports (like Padel or Fencing) to their personal inventory.

When a user adds a sport to their routine, should the platform default to a blank duration for them to fill in (e.g., entering "45 minutes" manually), or should the catalog offer pre-set blocks (e.g., "30-Min Run", "60-Min Run")?
